# Notification delivery options and provisioning constraints

Research date: 2026-11-11. Context: [Research notification delivery options and provisioning constraints](https://github.com/tkielar-vitesse/cohort-005-project/issues/3), under [Cadence notifications: find the way to a spec](https://github.com/tkielar-vitesse/cohort-005-project/issues/1).

This is evidence for later decisions, not a provider selection or a notification specification. No accounts were provisioned, credentials inspected, messages sent, or application code changed. Prices are public USD monthly list prices observed today, excluding tax, transport charges and contracts; recheck before purchasing. **Fact** means documented behavior or inspected repository code; **inference** means a consequence for a possible Cadence design; **unknown** means a choice or verification still needed.

## Existing application: facts and boundary

At repository commit `08ed53f`, [mailer.server.ts](../../app/lib/mailer.server.ts) sends plain-text messages using Postmark's HTTP API and the `outbound` stream when `POSTMARK_SERVER_TOKEN` is present. Otherwise it writes JSON files under `.outbox`. It throws on a failed HTTP status and has no durable queue, retry loop, provider message-ID persistence, or preference checks. Refund routes await sending after the database work: [student request](../../app/routes/courses.$slug.refund.ts) and [admin decision](../../app/routes/admin.refunds.tsx). These files are evidence of current behavior, not a complete proposed event catalogue.

[package.json](../../package.json) establishes React 19, React Router on Node, TypeScript, Drizzle and SQLite. [README](../../README.md) mentions a free Knock Developer account as course setup context. Inspection of [schema.ts](../../app/db/schema.ts) finds no persistent notification inbox/preference tables. Sonner action toasts are not a stored inbox.

**Inference:** notification orchestration (event routing, preferences, delayed work and feed storage) and email transport are separate decisions. Both managed options below can use Postmark, so retaining Postmark does not require application-owned orchestration. None of these services makes a database mutation and a remote workflow trigger atomic; reliable event handoff remains an application concern.

## Reminders, time zones and cancellation

**Knock facts:** one-off UTC schedules and recurring recipient-timezone-aware schedules exist. Missing recipient timezone falls back to account timezone or UTC. Recurring minutes use five-minute increments. Schedules can be listed, updated and deleted; deleting cancels enqueued occurrences. The docs distinguish deleting a future schedule from canceling an already invoked workflow. [Schedules](https://docs.knock.app/concepts/schedules)

Delays support fixed/dynamic intervals and timestamps, with a maximum of 365 days. A cancellation key allows canceling an open delay. Recipient properties/preferences are refreshed at each step; already-running workflows retain the workflow version from trigger time. [Delay function](https://docs.knock.app/designing-workflows/delay-function)

**Novu facts:** delays can target a future ISO timestamp or subscriber-local calendar time, defaulting to UTC without a timezone. A subscriber delivery schedule can defer or skip out-of-window delivery; extending a delay to the next window is an explicit setting, off by default. In-app and critical-workflow behavior differs from external channels. The Delay documentation states duration limits of 1 day Free, 7 days Pro, 30 days Team. [Delay step](https://docs.novu.co/platform/workflow/add-and-configure-steps/configure-action-steps/delay) Pending dynamic delays can be canceled with the trigger transaction ID. [Dynamic delay announcement](https://www.novu.co/changelog/dynamic-delay/)

**Postmark fact:** Postmark sends on request and does not schedule future delivery; scheduling must live in the calling system. [Official migration guide](https://postmarkapp.com/migration-guides/mailgun)

**Inference:** a possible learning reminder needs an application-owned eligibility rule and cancellation identity whichever orchestration is chosen. Completing a lesson, losing enrollment, changing a deadline/timezone, or opting out should be evaluated against the eventual policy before sending. A schedule is not proof that the recipient remains eligible. Already accepted email cannot be recalled through cancellation of future work. Application-owned scheduling would require durable jobs, a running worker/cron mechanism, clock/timezone handling, cancellation checks and restart recovery; none exists in the inspected mail adapter.

**Unknown/verification:** expected scheduling horizon, recurring versus one-off behavior, daylight-saving gaps/overlaps, timezone changes while queued, late/missed sends, completion/cancellation races, and freshness of preference evaluation in Novu's delayed path. Its pricing table and Delay documentation present potentially different Team delay limits; obtain the actual account entitlement before relying on a long delay. These need narrow acceptance scenarios after product decisions, not invented requirements now.

## Reliability: retries are not interchangeable

**Knock facts:** sending to providers is retried up to eight times over 30 minutes for transient errors, rate limits and network failures, with exponential backoff/jitter. Bounces and permanent errors are terminal; undelivered messages can be manually retried. [Delivery pipeline](https://docs.knock.app/send-notifications/delivering-notifications) Trigger idempotency uses `Idempotency-Key`, caches successful responses for 24 hours, and currently applies only to the workflow-trigger endpoint; different parameters with the same key are rejected. [API overview](https://docs.knock.app/api-reference/overview)

**Novu facts:** current retry documentation says external provider sends are attempted **once**, including provider 429/503 failures. Failures are recorded and emit `messages.failed`; the workflow continues to the next step. Framework bridge and outbound webhook retries are separate mechanisms. There is no automatic provider failover or dead-letter replay queue. [Delivery retry policy](https://docs.novu.co/platform/developer/delivery-retries)

Novu's API-layer `Idempotency-Key` capability may require support enablement for an organization. It supports POST/PATCH with API-key auth; success/error caches last 24 hours and in-progress keys five minutes. The documentation explicitly distinguishes this from `transactionId`, whose processing-time uniqueness check does not provide equivalent deduplication/billing protection. [Idempotency](https://docs.novu.co/api-reference/idempotency)

**Postmark facts:** the inspected adapter simply throws on non-success; its `.outbox` is a local message capture, not a delivery queue. Postmark emits delivery/bounce and other webhooks; temporary webhook failures are retried, so consumers must tolerate duplicate events. This is distinct from retrying an application's send request. [Webhooks](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/webhooks/webhooks-overview), [duplicate handling](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/what-are-webhooks-explained)

**Inference:** all options need stable business event IDs, durable handoff and an explicit recovery policy. Retrying a whole multi-channel workflow after one channel fails can repeat successful channels. An ambiguous timeout after email acceptance is not solved merely by retrying. Provider acceptance, inbox storage, delivery to the recipient's mail server, and user reading are different milestones. This investigation establishes no end-to-end exactly-once guarantee. A Postmark send-endpoint idempotency guarantee was not established from reviewed sources; do not assume one.

## Security, data boundaries and runtime fit

**Facts:** Knock client integrations use a public key plus signed user token when enhanced security is enabled. Enhanced security is off by default; without it a public key can act as any user. Backend secret keys belong on the server. [Client authentication](https://docs.knock.app/in-app-ui/security-and-authentication) Knock account documentation currently describes EU residency as forthcoming by end of 2026, not available evidence of an existing EU deployment. [Plans/account documentation](https://docs.knock.app/manage-your-account/knock-plans)

Novu requires enabling Inbox HMAC and generating the subscriber signature server-side to prevent users reading another subscriber's feed; context also requires a signature when used. [Prepare for production](https://docs.novu.co/platform/inbox/prepare-for-production) Novu offers US and EU regions; keys/endpoints are region-bound, and switching regions requires vendor involvement. Its documented Inbox retention is 30 days Free and 90 days Pro/Team; activity logs have separate shorter retention. [Security and retention](https://docs.novu.co/platform/additional-resources/security)

Postmark documents primary hosting near Chicago and AWS, and default message-content/activity retention of 45 days, adjustable through an add-on. [Hosting](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/is-postmark-secure-and-redundant), [Retention](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/how-does-the-retention-add-on-work)

**Inference:** managed orchestration adds a processor holding recipient identifiers, channel addresses, content/payloads, feed state, preferences and operational logs. Application-owned feeds keep those feed records in Cadence, while email content still crosses to Postmark. Novu EU hosting plus Postmark transport is not an entirely EU-only data path. Minimize event payloads and reauthorize notification links in Cadence; a feed token must be issued from the authenticated session, not an arbitrary client user ID. Retention/export/deletion and contractual requirements must be agreed, including backup/log behavior, before claiming a compliant boundary.

Both managed options document React/JS client support and Node/TypeScript backend examples ([Knock API](https://docs.knock.app/api-reference/overview), [Novu trigger API](https://docs.novu.co/api-reference/events/trigger-event)). **Inference:** these fit the repository's broad stack; exact package versions, React 19 peer compatibility, server rendering, browser subscriptions and build behavior remain untested. HTTP APIs allow a server integration without adopting a vendor workflow framework. No running integration or SDK compatibility test was performed.

**Conditional inference, not a volume forecast:** if `E` eligible recipient events each produce one feed item and one email, Knock usage is approximately `2E` successful channel messages; Novu usage is approximately `E` subscriber workflow runs; Postmark processes approximately `E` emails in either case. App-owned orchestration removes the orchestration subscription but adds engineering, database, worker hosting, monitoring and maintenance costs. Existing hosting capacity and operator time are unknown, so it cannot be declared free or cheaper.

## Inputs still needed for selection and a provisioning decision

1. Actual event/recipient catalogue, required channels, preference exceptions and whether team-specific settings are necessary.
2. Monthly recipient fan-out, channel mix, workflow count, peak rates, test usage, expected growth and approved spend; then estimate orchestration and transport separately.
3. Inbox lifetime, read/archive behavior, realtime expectation, accessibility/branding requirements and acceptable outages.
4. Reminder horizon, timezone ownership, quiet hours, cancellation/eligibility rules, lateness policy and recovery expectations. Validate the exact Novu plan delay limit and Knock schedule granularity against the chosen examples.
5. Required processing region, permitted payload fields, deletion/export behavior and agreement review; obtain confirmation rather than treating marketing compliance claims as a product guarantee.
6. Account ownership/access, available sender domain/DNS, test-recipient policy and whether service quotas suffice without billing. Only then provision development/test resources and verify SDK/runtime fit, signed feed access, preferences, restart/retry/cancellation scenarios, and sandbox webhooks.

These are decision inputs, not additional requirements or a recommendation to adopt a provider. The evidence supports three feasible architectural directions, with materially different operational ownership, retry semantics, data locations and cost units. The provider and product scope remain open.
